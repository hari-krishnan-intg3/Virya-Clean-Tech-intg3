### Post #014: Liquid-Cooled Charging Cables: The Physics of 500A Fast Charging
- **Pillar:** `Fleet Electrification & Megawatt Depots`
- **Target Audience:** `Electrical Engineers & EV Infrastructure Directors`
- **Visual Asset Brief:** *Cross-sectional 3D render of a liquid-cooled charging cable showing internal copper conductors, coolant circulation channels, and temperature sensors.*

```text
Why do high-power EV chargers need coolant fluid running through the charging cable?
Because copper physics has a thermal breaking point.

When you push high electrical currents through a charging cable, Joule heating (\(I^2 R\)) generates intense thermal losses inside the conductor.

• At 200A (standard DC charging): An air-cooled copper cable requires a cross-section of ~50 mm² and weighs around 2.5 kg/meter—stiff, but manageable for a human operator.
• At 500A (ultra-fast charging): An uncooled cable would require an enormous 150 mm² copper conductor, making the cable as thick as an elephant's trunk and weighing over 7 kg/meter—impossible for a driver to handle.

How do we deliver 500A through a lightweight, ergonomic cable?

Liquid Cooling.

A specialized synthetic dielectric coolant (or glycol-water mix) is pumped through internal micro-channels directly surrounding the copper conductors, pulling heat away to an integrated radiator in the charger cabinet.

This keeps cable surface temperatures below 50°C (compliant with IEC 61851-23 safety standards) while keeping the cable flexible, slim, and easy to handle.

💡 Key Takeaway: Without active liquid cooling, sustained ultra-fast charging is mechanically impossible. Cooling physics enables ergonomic 350kW+ charging.
```
